The one-minute version

  1. The first two emails went out on the night of August 26 to 255 people: 51 past customers and 204 shops and event planners.
  2. Store visits jumped the days after, and 14 people got all the way to the checkout page. None finished buying. That’s the biggest fixable thing on this page.
  3. The email system is locked because a $66.25 bill from August 19 hasn’t been paid. The three follow-up emails are stuck until it is.
  4. Social posting ran for five weeks, then stopped on August 20. 150 posts, about 28,000 video views, and it was picking up speed when the queue ran out. Nothing has posted for two weeks.
  5. Your first wholesale order came through Faire on August 10 for $164.40.

Two things need attention today

1. The email system (HubSpot) is locked. A $66.25 payment was due on August 19. It hasn’t been paid, so HubSpot has frozen the account. While it’s frozen, no emails can go out and we can’t see how many people opened or clicked the ones already sent. Paying the bill unlocks everything immediately.

2. Nothing has posted to Facebook or Instagram since August 20. The scheduling tool that posts for you has an empty calendar. Two weeks of silence after five weeks of growth is momentum lost, and it is easy to restart.

A fix that happened along the way: your emails now pass the spam check

The August 12 preview went straight to spam. The reason: your web address, mokipops.com, had not been set up to tell email providers “yes, HubSpot is allowed to send mail on my behalf.” Without that permission slip, Gmail and Outlook treat the mail as suspicious. That permission has now been added, and the August 20 re-send landed in the inbox, sent from moreflavor@mokipops.com. Every email since then has had the same protection.

What we can’t see until the bill is paid

How many people opened each email, how many clicked, and any replies or wholesale form submissions logged inside HubSpot. Those reports are behind the lock. They’ll be added to this page as soon as it opens. In the meantime, replies will be sitting in the moreflavor@mokipops.com mailbox.

Did the emails do anything? Yes.

We can see the effect in your online store’s visitor numbers even with HubSpot locked.

On an ordinary day in August the store got around 30 to 40 visits. On August 26 through 30, right after the emails, that jumped to roughly 120 to 130 visits on two separate days. Nothing else was running that week: no posts, no ads. The emails did that.

  • 33 people watched the announcement video on mokipops.com/pages/watch. That page is only linked from the emails, so every one of those was an email reader.
  • 22 people visited the wholesale page on the store, nearly triple the previous period.
  • 19 people put pops in their cart and 14 went to the checkout page, both up about half on the previous two months.
  • 0 finished buying.
What this means: the emails got people interested enough to shop. Something at the very last step is turning them away. That is almost always a shipping surprise (a big cost that only appears at checkout, or a “we don’t ship to your area” message) and it is usually a quick fix once we know which one it is. See next steps.

One bad day in July, explained

88 scheduled posts failed to publish. 83 of those were on a single day, July 16, when too many posts were queued at once and Facebook cut them off (it allows 35 posts per page per day). The other five were video files Facebook or Instagram couldn’t process. Nothing has failed since August 2. That July 16 pile-up is why the schedule was changed to one steady post a day.

Social brought attention, not clicks

Instagram sent 32 visitors to the store, Facebook 6, LinkedIn 1. That’s tiny next to 27,854 views, and it’s normal: Instagram doesn’t let people click links in captions. Social’s job is to make people recognise MOKIPOPS. The emails and Faire are what turn that into orders. The numbers show each channel doing its own job.

Words used on this page

HubSpot
The system that stores your customer and prospect list and sends the marketing emails.
Scheduling tool
Software that holds a calendar of posts and publishes them to Facebook and Instagram at set times, so nobody has to post by hand every day.
Faire
An online marketplace where independent shops order wholesale from brands like MOKIPOPS. Faire handles the payment and takes a commission.
Visit
One person opening your online store once. The same person coming back tomorrow counts as another visit.
Reached checkout
A shopper put something in the cart and got to the page where they enter payment and delivery details.
Views
How many times a video was watched. One person can watch more than once.
People who saw a post
The number of different people a post appeared in front of, counted once each. Also called “reach.”
Spam check
Email providers like Gmail check whether the sender is really allowed to send from that address. If the check fails, the email goes to spam. Yours now passes.
Preview / test send
A copy of an email sent only to your own inbox to make sure it looks right before real customers get it.
Automated visits
Computer programs, not people, loading your website. Common and harmless, but they inflate visitor counts, which is why we set the Iowa number aside.
